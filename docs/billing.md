# Billing: subscriptions

How subscriptions to a plan are billed, and what happens when a payment does not go through. Subscriptions use **Laravel Cashier** and Stripe; the shop's orders (products, gift cards) are a separate flow described in [commerce.md](commerce.md), and what a plan gives an account is in [entitlements.md](entitlements.md#subscription-plans).

> **Status:** plans, subscribing, cancelling and resuming, invoices and the webhook archive have been there since the start. Added for 1.0: plans that give entitlements, what a customer is told and can do when a payment fails, free trials, promotion codes, and a link to Stripe's customer portal. **Still to come in the SaaS milestone:** a metered-usage example.

## When a payment fails

Stripe takes the payment for each renewal. When it cannot, it tries again on a schedule you set in Stripe (**Settings → Billing → Revenue recovery**: how many retries, how far apart, and what happens when they run out: cancel the subscription or leave it unpaid). The shop does not decide any of that; it follows what Stripe's webhooks say, and tells the customer.

**What the customer loses.** By Cashier's default, a subscription that is **past due** (or unpaid, or incomplete) does not count as live, so **what its plan gives is paused from the first failed payment** and comes back when the payment goes through. This is a decision, not an accident: the shop does not give away what it is not being paid for while Stripe keeps trying. To keep access while Stripe retries instead, call `Cashier::keepPastDueSubscriptionsActive()` in a service provider; the emails and the billing page then say the plan stays active. Even then it is paused once Stripe gives up (the subscription becomes unpaid or is cancelled), and they say so: the page says "paused" whenever the subscription is not live, which is exactly what decides whether its plan gives anything.

**What the customer is sent.** Three emails, each sent once:

| When | The email |
|---|---|
| A renewal payment fails (`invoice.payment_failed`) | *We couldn't take your payment*: the amount Stripe tried to take (`amount_due`, which is less than the invoice total when a credit or balance came off first) and the plan, that their plan is paused, when Stripe will try again (or that it will not), and a button to the billing page. Sent for **each** failed attempt, with *We still couldn't take your payment* from the second. |
| The payment goes through after having failed (`invoice.payment_succeeded`) | *Payment received, thank you*: the amount paid (`amount_paid`) and that the plan is active again. Not sent for an ordinary payment that never failed. |
| Stripe ends the subscription for want of payment (`customer.subscription.deleted` with the reason `payment_failed`) | *Your subscription has ended*, with a link to choose a plan again. A subscription the customer cancelled is not blamed on payment. |

Only a **subscription's** invoice is chased: one that names its subscription (`parent.subscription_details.subscription`, or a top-level `subscription` on a webhook endpoint set to an older Stripe API version). A manual or one-off invoice failing for the same customer is not. The very first payment of a new subscription is not chased either: the customer is on the page that asked for it, which says what went wrong.

Stripe does not promise to deliver events in order. A failure that arrives **after** the invoice was paid is stale: it does not mark the paid invoice failed and it does not email the customer, who has paid (it is still kept in the webhook archive).

Each notice is **claimed before it is sent**, in `billing_dunning_notices` (one row per kind, invoice or subscription, and attempt). Stripe delivers a webhook again when it is not answered in time, and an admin can replay one from **Billing → Webhooks**; neither tells the customer twice. If the email cannot be queued the claim is undone, so Stripe's next delivery of the same event tries again.

**What the customer can do.** The billing settings show a red notice while a payment is behind, with **Update payment method** and **Pay now**. Stripe does *not* try again when a card is changed, only on its schedule, so without **Pay now** a customer who had put their card right would wait, with their plan paused, for the next attempt. It asks Stripe to pay the subscription's latest invoice with the card on file and, if that works, brings the plan back at once rather than waiting for the webhook. It says the plan is back only when the subscription, as Stripe then reports it, is no longer behind and is live. If Stripe took the request but the money has not arrived (a bank debit takes days) they are told the payment is being processed, and the plan comes back when Stripe confirms it. If the card is declined the page says why (Stripe's own message). If the bank wants the customer (3-D Secure) they are sent to Stripe's page for the invoice. If Stripe cannot be reached they are asked to try again, and the error is reported. It is limited to six presses a minute.

## Free trials

A plan can give a new customer **free days before the first payment**: **Billing → Plans**, the *Free trial (days)* field (empty is no trial; up to 730, which is Stripe's limit; none by default). It applies to people who subscribe from then on.

- **The card is taken up front.** The customer adds a card as for any subscription. Stripe charges nothing until the trial ends and then takes the first payment; if that payment fails it is followed up like any other (see above).
- **One trial per customer.** Someone who has ever had a subscription of any kind (running, cancelled or ended) is charged from the start, so cancelling and coming back is not a way to another trial. A visitor with no account yet is eligible. (Someone who deletes their account and signs up again is a new customer, which cannot be told apart.) The check is made while the subscription is being started, under the per-user lock that deleting the account also takes, so two sign-ups at once cannot both get a trial.
- **The plan works from the first day.** A trial counts as live, so what the plan gives (its entitlements) is given for the whole of it.
- **The pages say so.** The pricing page and Billing settings show *N-day free trial* on the plan, *Start free trial* instead of *Start subscription*, and that the card is not charged until it ends, but only to someone who is eligible. Billing settings show *Free trial until* a date, and *Cancel trial*.
- **Cancelling during the trial** ends it on the day it would have ended, and nothing is ever charged.
- **A reminder before it ends.** Stripe sends `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` three days before the trial ends; the subscriber is emailed *Your free trial ends soon*: the date, the plan's **regular price** after it, and that they can cancel before then. The email does not promise a final amount, because only Stripe's invoice knows it (a discount on the subscription, or tax, changes it): it says that any discount is taken off and that anything left to pay is charged to the card. Sent once (the same claim table as the payment emails), and not for a trial that has been cancelled to end by the time it does, or that has already ended. A subscription set to be cancelled *later* (after the trial and a payment or two) is still warned, since the first payment is still taken. A trial shorter than three days is warned about at once, by Stripe.

## Promotion codes

A customer can type a **promotion code** when they subscribe (Billing settings, and the pricing page once the payment form is loaded). The codes are made and kept **in Stripe**, not here: in the Stripe Dashboard make a coupon (a percentage or an amount, taken off once, for some months, or on every payment) and give it a customer-facing code (*Use customer-facing promotion codes*). Expiry, redemption limits, one particular customer, first-time customers only and a minimum first payment are all set there too. Nothing about a code is stored in this app. (Stripe's words: the *coupon* is the discount, the *promotion code* is the word the customer types.)

- **It is checked before anybody pays.** *Apply* asks Stripe about the code for the chosen plan, and says what it takes off ("20% off for 3 months", "5.00 USD off your first payment") or why it cannot be used: Stripe has no such live code (it does not say whether it never existed, expired, ran out or was switched off, so neither can the shop); it was made for another customer; it is a flat amount in another currency than the plan's; it is limited to other products (Stripe would accept it and take nothing off, so the shop refuses it); or it is a flat amount that lasts forever (Cashier does not allow that on a subscription). Choosing another plan checks the code again, and an answer that arrives after the code or the plan has been changed is thrown away. The same code can be made for any number of particular customers at Stripe, so when there are more than a page of them the shop asks Stripe for this customer's own instead of paging through everybody's.
- **Conditions Stripe holds it to** (first-time customers only, a minimum first payment) are shown under the code as notes and are **not** judged beforehand. If Stripe refuses the code when the subscription is started, the customer is told *That code can't be used on this subscription. Nothing has been charged.* and nothing is started.
- **It is looked up again at the end.** Starting a subscription checks the code once more, whatever the page found earlier (it may have run out meanwhile), and before the card is touched. A code that was typed but never applied still counts, and is refused there if it will not do.
- **Guessing is slowed down.** Ten tries a minute and sixty an hour per person, right or wrong, because each one is a question put to Stripe. Checking a code and starting a subscription with one **share** that allowance (starting one looks the code up too, so it would otherwise be a way round the hourly limit). Starting a subscription with no code is not counted, and has the pricing page's limit of ten a minute.
- **With a free trial,** the code goes on the subscription as it starts, and Stripe decides which invoice a once-only discount lands on and how a trial counts against "for N months". Try a code on a plan with a trial in Stripe's test mode before running a campaign with it.
- **The subscribe requests changed.** They used to take a raw Stripe coupon id as `coupon`; they take `promotion_code` (the code a customer types) now, on both `settings/billing/subscribe` and `pricing/subscribe`. Checking a code is `settings/billing/promotion-code` and `pricing/promotion-code`. Starting a subscription on the billing page also now refuses a plan that is not for sale, as the pricing page already did.

## Customer portal

The billing page has a **Manage billing in Stripe** button that sends the customer to **Stripe's hosted billing portal**, where they can update their card, see their invoices and, as far as you allow, change or cancel their subscription. Stripe hosts and runs the portal; nothing is built here beyond opening it.

- **Set it up once in Stripe, for each mode.** Stripe Dashboard → Settings → Billing → **Customer portal**: choose what customers may do, and **save**. Until the settings are saved in a mode, Stripe refuses to open a portal in it: the customer is told *We couldn't open the billing portal just now* and the reason is in the log (*Stripe would not open a billing portal session*). If you made more than one portal configuration, put the id (`bpc_...`) of the one to use in `STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION`; otherwise Stripe uses the default.
- **Who sees the button.** A customer who has a billing account at Stripe (it is made when they subscribe or add a card), when Stripe is set up here (`STRIPE_SECRET`) and the portal is on. `BILLING_PORTAL_ENABLED=false` removes the button and the route (it answers 404), for a site that does not want customers cancelling or switching plans anywhere but the shop's own pages.
- **It comes back to the billing page.** The return address is fixed on the server and is never taken from the request, and Stripe's address is only followed if it is `https`. The button is a POST that makes a session each time, limited like the other billing requests (ten a minute).
- **What the portal changes reaches the shop through the webhook.** A card, a cancellation or a plan switch made there arrives as `customer.subscription.updated` or `.deleted` (events already needed, see below) and the subscription here follows, so what a plan gives follows the **Stripe price** the subscription has, as for any change. Until the delivery arrives the billing page may still show the old state. A plan switch needs the portal to allow it and the other plans' prices to be listed there; a subscription moved to a price that no plan here carries gives nothing (see [entitlements](entitlements.md#subscription-plans)).

## Webhook events

One endpoint, `POST /stripe/webhook`, signed with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, for subscriptions and for shop orders. The subscription side needs these events switched on in Stripe: `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated` and `customer.subscription.deleted` (Cashier keeps the local subscription in step with them), `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` (the trial reminder), and `invoice.payment_succeeded` and `invoice.payment_failed` (invoices, and the emails above).

## What is stored

| Table | |
|---|---|
| `subscription_plans` | The plans, with their Stripe price, what they give and the days of free trial they offer (`trial_days`). |
| `subscriptions`, `subscription_items` | Cashier's copy of each subscription. A subscription has no foreign key to its user, so one can outlive its user (deleting an account ends its Stripe subscriptions first, but the row can stay; the webhook archive copes: an event about one is kept without the user). |
| `billing_invoices` | One row per Stripe invoice, written from the invoice webhooks. |
| `billing_webhook_calls` | The archive of every delivery, replayable from the ACP. |
| `billing_dunning_notices` | Which payment and trial emails were sent, so that each is sent once (`kind`: `failed`, `recovered`, `cancelled`, `trial_ending`). |

Needs `php artisan migrate`; no new permission.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| A customer says they got no email about a failed payment | Check **Billing → Webhooks** for the `invoice.payment_failed` delivery: if it is missing, the event is not switched on in Stripe or the endpoint is not reaching the shop. If it is there, the mail queue (`mail`) may not be running. Replaying it does not resend an email that was already sent. |
| **Manage billing in Stripe** says *We couldn't open the billing portal just now* | Look in the log for *Stripe would not open a billing portal session*: usually the portal's settings were never saved in the Stripe Dashboard for this mode (test and live are separate), or `STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION` names a configuration that does not exist. |
| A customer has no **Manage billing in Stripe** button | They have no billing account at Stripe yet (it is made when they subscribe or add a card), `STRIPE_SECRET` is not set, or `BILLING_PORTAL_ENABLED` is false. |
| A customer changed their plan in the portal and the billing page still shows the old one | The webhook has not arrived yet. Check **Billing → Webhooks** for the `customer.subscription.updated` delivery. |
| A customer says their code is refused | *Apply* gives the reason when the shop can see one. *That code isn't valid, or it has expired* means Stripe has no live code by that name for them: check it in the Stripe Dashboard (active, not past its expiry or redemption limit, and made for everybody or for that customer). The shop cannot tell more than Stripe says. |
| *We couldn't check that code just now* | Stripe could not be reached or answered with an error (the log has *Could not check a promotion code with Stripe*). The customer can try again, or remove the code and subscribe without it. |
| A code was accepted but the invoice shows no discount | Look at the subscription's discounts in Stripe. A once-only discount is gone from the subscription once an invoice has used it, and with a free trial Stripe decides which invoice that is. |
| A customer was not offered the trial | They have had a subscription before (a trial is for first-time subscribers), or the plan has no trial days. The pages show the trial only to someone eligible. |
| A customer got no trial reminder | `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` is not switched on in Stripe, or the trial was cancelled to end with it (one set to end later is still warned) or had already ended, or it was shorter than three days (Stripe then warns at once, when it starts). Check **Billing → Webhooks** for the delivery; replaying it does not resend a reminder that was sent. |
| A customer got no email for the first payment of a new subscription | By design: they are on the checkout page, which says so. |
| A customer's plan is paused though their card is fine | Their subscription is still past due: the card was updated but nothing asked Stripe to pay, so they should press **Pay now** (or wait for Stripe's next attempt). |
| **Pay now** says the payment is being processed | Stripe accepted the request but the payment is not confirmed yet (a bank debit, or Stripe has not updated the subscription). The plan comes back when the webhook says so; nothing more to do. |
| **Pay now** says Stripe could not be reached | A network or key problem on the shop's side, reported in the logs. Nothing was charged. |
| An email says "will try again on" a date that has passed | Stripe tried again and failed again (a new email follows) or has not had a chance to; the date is the one Stripe gave when the event was sent. |

## Testing

`tests/Feature/Billing/DunningTest.php` covers the three emails (the wording, once each, replays, the attempts, the first payment, unknown customers, a failure to send). `tests/Feature/Billing/PayNowTest.php` covers **Pay now** through the real Stripe SDK with the network replaced by scripted answers (`tests/Support/ScriptedStripeHttpClient.php`): paid, declined, 3-D Secure, an invoice Stripe already took, nothing to pay, Stripe unreachable, and the button and the page. `tests/Feature/Database/BillingDunningNoticesMigrationTest.php` covers the table. `tests/Feature/Billing/TrialsTest.php` covers who gets a trial and starting one through the real Stripe SDK with scripted answers, `TrialReminderTest.php` the reminder, `tests/Feature/Admin/SubscriptionPlanTrialTest.php` choosing the days in the ACP, and `tests/Feature/Database/PlanTrialDaysMigrationTest.php` the column. `tests/Feature/Billing/PromotionCodesTest.php` covers the lookup through the real Stripe SDK (`tests/Support/RoutedStripeHttpClient.php` answers each call by what it is): what a code says, who may use it, the plan it suits, what Stripe holds it to and Stripe being down; `PromotionCodeSubscribingTest.php` covers starting a subscription with a code, Stripe's refusals, the pages' requests and the limits. `tests/Feature/Billing/CustomerPortalTest.php` covers opening the portal through the real Stripe SDK (the customer and return address sent, the configuration, Stripe refusing or being down, an address that is not secure), who is offered it, the switch and the limit.
