# Changelog

All notable changes to MetaForge are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

The path to 1.0.0 is laid out in [docs/ROADMAP-v1.0.0.md](docs/ROADMAP-v1.0.0.md).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- A `docs/ROADMAP-v1.0.0.md` release plan covering features, security, performance, payments (Stripe and Tebex), search and the milestones to 1.0.0.
- Authorised download route for support-ticket attachments (web and API) with a policy, plus a `support:attachments:privatize` command.
- CI jobs that run the test suite on MySQL 8 and PostgreSQL 16 in addition to SQLite, with a guard test that fails if a job runs on the wrong database.
- `SECURITY.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `.well-known/security.txt`, issue and pull-request templates, `CODEOWNERS` and Dependabot configuration.
- `compose.yaml` with the backing services for local development (MySQL, PostgreSQL, Redis, Meilisearch and Mailpit).
- Internationalisation infrastructure (English only for now): `config/i18n.php`, the `SetLocale` middleware, `useI18n()`, shared translations as Inertia props and `php artisan lang:check` (see `docs/i18n.md`).
- Static analysis with Larastan (level 5, with a baseline that shrinks as relations are typed), a gitleaks secrets scan, a weekly dependency-advisory audit and a scheduled workflow that repeats the test suite in random order.
- **Checkout and orders (M1, first slice).** Guests and signed-in customers can check out a cart with Stripe: an order is placed with its stock held, payment happens on hosted Stripe Checkout, and a webhook marks it paid and emails a receipt. Orders have a lifecycle (`pending`, `processing`, `completed`, `cancelled`) separate from payment state, unpaid orders expire and release their stock, and every stock change is recorded in an inventory ledger. A cart never has two payable checkouts: starting again closes the earlier Stripe session and confirms it before a replacement is created, and paying removes only the ordered lines from the cart. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md).
- A provider-neutral payment layer (`App\Payments`): a `PaymentProvider` contract, `PaymentManager`, `StripeProvider`, and a store-level provider setting in the ACP (System settings). Tebex plugs in behind the same contract in M2.
- Idempotent webhook handling shared by all providers: each delivery is stored under `(provider, external_id)`, redeliveries are acknowledged without reprocessing, and failures are recorded and retried by the provider.
- Cart quantity updates and removal, a checkout page, an order confirmation page (signed link for guests) and an owner-scoped order history.
- `Money` value object for exact arithmetic in minor units, including zero-decimal currencies, and `COMMERCE_*` configuration (`config/commerce.php`).
- Orders and payments belong to an owner (`owner_type`/`owner_id`) and are authorised through a policy, so teams can own orders in 1.1 without a rewrite.
- Factories for products, variants, prices, inventory, orders and payments.
- **Addresses, shipping and tax at checkout (M1, second slice).** Checkout collects a shipping address (and a billing address when it differs, or for digital goods when tax depends on location), offers the shipping methods that apply and shows shipping and tax as the shopper types. A signed-in customer has an address book (Settings → Addresses) and can pick or save addresses. Orders keep their own copy of both addresses. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md).
- Shipping zones and rates (by country, with a rest-of-world zone, minimum and maximum order values such as free shipping over a threshold) and a tax-rate table (country and region, several rates adding together, rate applying to shipping or not, products can be untaxed). With no zones configured, orders ship anywhere for free as before.
- `OrderPricer`, one pricing engine for the live quote and the order that is placed, working in exact minor units with the tax shared between lines by largest remainder (`Money::percent()` and `Money::allocate()`). Stripe is sent shipping and each tax as their own lines, and the shipping address is attached to the payment.
- Products can be marked as not needing shipping (digital goods) and as not taxable; the ACP product form sets both.
- Example shipping zones, rates and tax rates in the demo seeder.
- **Admin screens for shipping and tax.** Commerce → Shipping manages zones (pick countries one by one, add all EU countries, or cover everywhere else) and their rates (price, optional minimum and maximum order value, active or not); Commerce → Tax rates manages the tax table. The screens warn when shipping isn't set up or a zone has no active rates, and when you are about to delete your last active zone. Each action has its own permission (`commerce.acp.view`, `create`, `edit`, `delete`).
- **Order management and refunds (M1).** Commerce → Orders lists and searches orders (by status, payment state, number, name or email) and opens each one to show its items, totals, customer, addresses, payments and history. Staff can mark an order fulfilled with optional tracking (the customer is emailed), cancel an unpaid order, check a payment with the provider, and leave notes. Every change is written to an append-only order history with who did it.
- **Refunds.** Full or partial refunds sent back through Stripe from the order page, with a reason, a note, an option to return the items to stock and an option to email the customer. A refund is recorded before the provider is asked and is idempotent, so a double click or a lost reply cannot refund twice; a refund Stripe has not confirmed stays pending until it is checked, and a refund that fails frees its balance. Refunds made in the Stripe dashboard are picked up through the `refund.*` and `charge.refunded` webhooks, and a refund made outside the shop can be recorded by hand. Orders gain the payment states `partially_refunded` and `refunded`; a full refund of an unshipped order cancels it. Refunding has its own permission, `commerce.acp.refund`. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#refunds).
- **Catalogue management (M1).** Commerce → Products lists and searches products (by name, address or SKU) and opens each to edit everything in one place: details, brand, categories and tags; the price; options and variants; and stock. A checklist on the page says what is still missing before a product can be bought. Options can be renamed (the variants made from them follow) and a variant is made for every combination with one click. Prices follow one rule (a single active price in the shop's currency), so what a shopper pays is never ambiguous. Products and variants that have been ordered are archived or switched off rather than deleted, so order history and the stock ledger stay intact; everything else can be deleted and takes its prices, stock and cart lines with it.
- **Stock adjustments.** Staff can set a counted figure or add and remove stock with a note; each change is written to the movement ledger (reason `adjustment`) with who made it, and tracked stock that is running out is listed on the Commerce overview (`COMMERCE_LOW_STOCK_THRESHOLD`).
- **Product pictures and a real product page (M1).** A Pictures tab on each product takes several uploads at once, with descriptions, a main picture and an order. Every upload is decoded and encoded again as WebP at three sizes, so metadata and anything hidden in the file do not survive, anything that is not a picture is refused whatever it is called, and a file that declares a gigantic size is refused before it is decoded. The shop's catalogue now shows each product's picture and price (with savings), and the product page has a gallery, option pickers that follow the variants, the price and stock of the combination, and add to cart; the cart shows each line's picture. Stock is described in words ("Only a few left", "Out of stock", "Available to order"), never as a count, products that are sold out say so, and the page carries structured data with the pictures and an offer. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#product-pictures).
- **Discount codes (M1).** Staff create codes under Commerce → Discount codes: a percentage off, a fixed amount off or free shipping, with optional dates, a minimum spend, a total number of uses, a number of uses per customer, and the products and categories it applies to. Customers put a code on the cart or on the checkout page (guests too); it is checked again whenever the cart is priced and when the order is placed, so one definition of "valid" applies everywhere. The discount is shared between the eligible lines to the cent, tax is charged on what is left, and Stripe is sent line amounts that add up to exactly what the order says. Uses are counted from orders that have not been cancelled, so cancelling, expiring or refunding an order gives the use back, and two checkouts racing for the last use cannot both win. A code that saves nothing (a percentage that rounds to nothing, free shipping on shipping that is already free) is refused instead of using one of its uses, a code limited to products or categories keeps applying to nothing, not everything, if they are all deleted, and an order refunded in full gives its use back even after it was shipped. A code that orders have used can be switched off but not deleted, and what it has taken off is shown per currency. Applying codes is rate limited. The order, its confirmation page and email and the ACP order page show the code and the discount, and the demo seeder adds `WELCOME10`, `SAVE5` and `FREESHIP`. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#discount-codes).
- **Digital downloads and orders that cost nothing (M1).** A product can deliver files once it is paid for: staff upload them on a Downloads tab (kept privately under generated names, with a checksum and the type the server sees), and a paid order gives each line that has files the right to download them. The order page lists them with short-lived signed links, each file can be downloaded a limited number of times (and access can expire), downloads are spent atomically, and files are always sent as attachments. A refund in full ends the downloads and a failed one brings them back; staff can reset, revoke and restore from the order. An order made only of digital lines is completed as soon as it is paid. Orders that cost nothing (a price of 0, or a discount code that covers the whole order) are now paid on the spot without a payment provider. A new version of a file gives buyers their downloads of it again. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#digital-goods).
- **Licence keys (M1).** A product, or one of its variants, can sell keys (software licences, game codes, gift codes): staff switch it on from the product's Licence keys tab and paste keys in, one per line, and each unit bought is given its own key when the order is paid, shown on the customer's order page. The stock count follows the pool (unused keys, less what orders have spoken for), so the catalogue, cart and checkout sell keys without knowing about them and the last key cannot be sold twice; counting such stock by hand is refused, and every change is in the movement ledger. Keys are encrypted with `APP_KEY` (back it up), are never serialised, never sent to the ACP in full (staff see the end of a key), never put in an email, the order's history or a log, and the order page that shows them is never cached and has an encrypted browser history entry. A key that was handed over is never put back in the pool: a refund in full hides the order's keys (and brings them back if the refund fails), and returning the items to stock puts back only units that were not delivered. An order paid when the pool has nothing to give (a late payment) waits, says so to the customer and to staff, and is issued automatically, oldest first, when keys are added. Staff can withdraw or replace a customer's key from the order, and an unused key can be deleted only while the count can spare it. A product or variant that still holds keys cannot be deleted. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#licence-keys).
- **A dashboard that shows a customer their shop activity.** When the shop is on, the dashboard lists the customer's newest orders (status, total, what each holds) and a "Your keys and downloads" card that opens the order page where a licence key is copied or a file downloaded, with a note when a paid key is still on its way. Orders are found through whoever owns them, never through who placed them. The dashboard only ever receives how many keys an order holds, never a key: the keys stay on the order page, and it only mentions downloads while the order page would still offer a file (not once every file's allowance is used up). It also gains a plan card (subscription state, with a nudge when a payment failed or the plan is ending), an account card (two-factor status, reputation and badges, shortcuts to the shop, orders, addresses, billing, support, notifications and profile) and is rebuilt to leave out whatever the site owner has switched off (forum, support, blog or shop) and the author's card for people who write nothing. The order history now pages through every order (it showed the first ten) and shows the same key and download badges. Everything is worked out in a fixed number of queries however many orders there are.
- **A guest's cart and orders follow them into their account.** Signing in gives the browser a new session, which used to leave the guest's cart behind (registering too). It is now merged into the account's cart at the moment of signing in, by every way of signing in (password, two-factor step, social, registration and the pricing page), without a password alone handing anything over when there is a second step (and a cart filled in while the code is awaited comes along too). A checkout the guest had already started stays payable and carries on from the account's cart. Guest orders placed with an address are linked to the account once that address is verified (on verification, by the emailed link or by an administrator, and on each sign-in), with their payments, keys and downloads, and a guest looking at their own order is invited to make an account to keep it. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#a-guest-becoming-a-customer).
- **Wishlists and product reviews (M1).** A signed-in customer saves products with a heart on any product card or page and finds them on their own Wishlist page (header, dashboard and account links), shown with the same cards as the catalogue; up to `COMMERCE_WISHLIST_MAX` (200), private, and hidden while a product is switched off. Customers can review a product once with 1 to 5 stars, a headline and some plain text, and change or delete it; a review from someone with a paid order is marked *Verified purchase*, `COMMERCE_REVIEWS_APPROVAL` decides which reviews wait for staff (`unverified` by default: buyers publish at once, others wait), `COMMERCE_REVIEWS_REQUIRE_PURCHASE` can limit reviewing to buyers and `COMMERCE_REVIEWS=false` switches it off. A rejected review is kept and its writer cannot change or delete it. The product page shows the average, a bar for each star, sortable paged reviews and a form; the catalogue shows each product's rating; the page's structured data carries an `aggregateRating`; order history links each paid line to its product to review it. Staff moderate under **Commerce → Reviews** (waiting, published, rejected, search, rating filter; publish, reject and delete with the existing edit and delete permissions) and the overview counts what is waiting. Both are in the data export and go with the account. The catalogue cards are now one shared component and presenter (`StorefrontProducts`). See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#reviews).
- **Gift cards (M1).** Staff make gift cards under **Commerce → Gift cards** and give the code to a customer, who types it on the cart or checkout page. A card pays as much of an order as it holds, shipping and tax included (one card per order), and what is left stays on it. A gift card is tender, not a discount: the order is still worth what it cost, and what Stripe is asked for is the total less the card's share (Stripe is sent one line saying so). An order a card pays in full is paid on the spot with no payment provider. The code is shown once and never kept (only a SHA-256 hash and the last four characters are), a lost code can be replaced, and every change to a balance is in an append-only ledger written with the card's row locked, so two checkouts cannot overspend one card. The card's share is taken when the order is placed and given back if the order is cancelled, expires, is replaced or cannot be started; a payment that arrives after a cancellation takes it again and flags a shortfall. Refunds go back onto the card first (up to what it paid, even if it has since been switched off) and the rest the way the order was paid, as up to two refunds for one request with one email. Cards can expire and be switched off, staff can add or take away value with a reason, and `COMMERCE_GIFT_CARDS=false` hides the box. The order confirmation and receipt show what the card paid, the commerce overview shows what is owed on cards, and no new permission is needed. See [docs/commerce.md](docs/commerce.md#gift-cards).
- **Tebex catalogue (M2, first slice).** Set `TEBEX_PUBLIC_TOKEN` and the shop keeps a local copy of a Tebex store's categories and packages, refreshed by `php artisan tebex:sync`, by the scheduler (`TEBEX_SYNC_EVERY_MINUTES`, default 60) and by **Commerce → Tebex** in the ACP, which also tests the connection and lists the packages, the result of the last run and the last ten runs. A run is all or nothing, only one runs at a time, a failure is recorded with its reason and leaves the copy as it was, and a package Tebex stops listing is kept (stamped `removed_at`) so an order can still refer to it. The Headless API client times out, retries network failures, 5xx and 429, never puts the token in a message and never sends the private key where it is not needed. Prices are kept to the cent and descriptions are stored as Tebex wrote them, to be cleaned before they are shown. Tested without a network against fixtures written from Tebex's OpenAPI schema and webhook documentation; **buying through Tebex is not built yet** (baskets and checkout, webhooks, recurring payments and entitlements are the next slices). Needs `php artisan migrate`; no new permission. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md).
- **Tebex storefront, cart and checkout (M2, second slice).** While Tebex is the store's provider, `/shop` shows its packages (from the local copy: search, categories, paging, a page for each with its description cleaned and its price before tax), a cart holds packages (never together with products, all in one currency, one of a package that is not sold in quantities, a customer limit kept) and checkout places a pending order whose lines are the packages, makes a **basket** at Tebex (with where the buyer comes back to, the order's public id and a nonce as its custom data, the buyer's address, and each package by its ident) and sends the buyer to Tebex to pay. Tebex is the merchant of record, so the shop's own discount codes, gift cards, shipping and tax are not used with packages and no address is asked for; the order carries the price before tax and the payment records what Tebex quoted. A checkout the shop is done with (the buyer starts again, the window passes) is closed by taking the packages out of its basket, and is never replaced while it may have been paid. A call that changes something at Tebex is repeated only after a 429. The store's platform is remembered whenever Tebex is asked (a synchronisation, Test connection), and the shop sells only for a platform it knows how to take a buyer through (see the next entry): for any other the ACP page warns, the storefront and cart refuse, and no basket is made. Settling an order is the webhook's job (next entries). Needs `php artisan migrate` (cart and order lines gain a nullable `tebex_package_id`). A cart left over from the other kind (the provider can change while a cart waits) refuses what is added to it; a guest's cart that cannot share a cart with the account's when they sign in (products against packages, or packages in another currency) is left out with a notice instead of making a cart no checkout will take; and a package's limit holds when a quantity is edited, as when it is added. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md).
- **Selling for the games (M2, third slice).** A Tebex store is set up for a platform, and the platform decides what the buyer must do before they can pay; the shop now takes them through it. **Minecraft (Java, Bedrock, Offline/Geyser) and Overwolf:** the buyer types their username on the checkout page, it goes into the basket as it is made, and Tebex answers with the account it recognised (one it does not is sent back to the form, the order cancelled, nothing put in a basket). **Discord and the Steam-based games (FiveM, RedM, GTA V, Rust, ARK, Garry's Mod, Counter-Strike, 7 Days to Die and the rest):** Tebex will not take a package into a basket until the buyer has signed in, so checkout places the order and makes the basket, then sends the buyer to a signed link of the shop's own that sends them to Tebex's sign-in for that basket (a page lets them choose when Tebex offers several), and when they are back puts the packages in with their Tebex id and sends them to pay. The link works out how far the buyer has got from the basket itself, so it is safe to open twice or come back to later; a paid basket goes to the order page, an expired order to the cart. Packages are delivered to the account Tebex knows: it is kept on the payment, shown to the customer ("Delivered to") and to staff (name and id) on the order. Universal (No Auth) stores work as before; a platform the shop does not know (CurseForge, one Tebex adds later) is still refused. New `TebexStoreProfile` (the platform list, taken from Tebex's own SDK), `TebexSignIn`, `TebexBasketBuilder`, the `shop.tebex.continue` route and a sign-in page. Only one request at a time works on a basket (a lock per basket, from reading it to filling it, also taken when a checkout is closed; `TEBEX_BASKET_LOCK_WAIT` seconds to wait, default 10), so a sign-in link opened in two tabs cannot put every package in twice; and a basket the shop will not sell on (no place to pay, another currency) sends the buyer back to the cart with a message instead of an error page. No migration. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#what-buyers-are-asked).
- **Tebex webhooks (M2, fourth slice).** Tebex tells the shop a payment happened at `POST /webhooks/tebex`, and the shop marks an order paid from nothing else. The endpoint answers only Tebex's two addresses (404 for others; `TEBEX_WEBHOOK_CHECK_IP=false` for a proxy that hides them), checks `X-Signature` (HMAC-SHA256 of the body's SHA-256, constant-time, of the body exactly as sent) and answers the validation delivery with its own id. Deliveries are kept in the Stripe webhook archive under Tebex's own id (so a repeat changes nothing and a failure is retried), with the buyer's email, name, address, IP and gift card numbers removed. `payment.completed` finds its order by the basket's custom data (the order's public id **and** the nonce on its payment; a wrong nonce pays nothing), marks it paid at what Tebex says the buyer paid (more than the shop's quote is recorded as tax, less as a discount), takes the package out of the cart and completes the order, since Tebex delivers it; a payment in another currency or with no amount is put in review, not applied; so is a payment for other packages than the order holds (the basket can be changed by anyone with its identifier, so a cheaper package paid for must not complete a dearer order: the ids and total quantities must match exactly); a second payment for a settled basket is flagged, never applied; a payment that arrives after the order expired is honoured. `payment.declined` is noted for staff, `payment.refunded` and a lost dispute are recorded as refunds (never twice), other disputes are noted, and recurring payment deliveries are kept and not acted on yet. A delivery the shop could not act on shows as "Needs a look" in Billing → Webhooks (now filterable by sender, with a Tebex replay) and on the Tebex page, which also shows the endpoint and whether the secret is set. **`TEBEX_CHECKOUT_PREVIEW` is gone:** Tebex is always an available provider and says it is not configured until `TEBEX_WEBHOOK_SECRET` is set. Not yet checked against a real store (M2b): where Tebex returns the basket's custom data. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#payments-the-webhook).
- **Tebex embedded checkout (M2, fifth slice).** `TEBEX_CHECKOUT_MODE=popup` or `inline` keeps the buyer on the shop's own page to pay, with [Tebex.js](https://docs.tebex.io/developers/tebex.js/overview) (`redirect`, Tebex's own page, stays the default and the fallback). Checkout sends the buyer to the shop's signed link, which reads the basket and shows a payment page with a Pay now button (popup) or Tebex's checkout inside it (inline); a store that makes buyers sign in still does that first. **Nothing the browser says settles an order:** `payment:complete` only takes the buyer to the order page, which waits for the webhook, and a payment error or a closed popup leaves the order open. If Tebex.js cannot be used (a blocker, a failed download) the page says so and offers Tebex's own page for the same basket, which is also offered beneath it whatever happens. Tebex.js is bundled from the `@tebexio/tebex.js` npm package (pinned in `package-lock.json`, MIT, with `zoid` and helpers under Apache-2.0: eight packages for the licence audit) and loaded only on this page, as a separate file, so no script comes from another site and no other page carries it. The content security policy lets `https://pay.tebex.io` in as a frame only while the mode is embedded (`tebex.checkout.frame_sources`). The checkout button says "Continue to payment" instead of "Continue to Tebex" when embedded. Also: every package is now added to a basket with the order's id and nonce as its own `custom` data, which Tebex documents as returned in the payment's webhook (the place the sample payment shows it), alongside the basket's; and an address to pay at must be `https://`. `TEBEX_CHECKOUT_THEME` picks Tebex.js's colour theme. Needs `npm install` and a front-end build. Not yet checked against a real store (M2b): the popup and inline frame for real baskets, `payment:complete`, and inline checkout on a phone. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#paying-on-the-shops-own-page-tebexjs).
- **Tebex recurring payments (M2, sixth slice).** A recurring package is paid at checkout and then again every period by Tebex, which charges, retries and ends it and tells the shop by webhook; the shop now keeps a copy of what it was told and records each renewal. The first payment (settled as before) names the recurring payment it started, and from then on the order in the ACP shows where it stands (active, payment overdue with the failed tries, ending at the end of the period, cancelled or expired, and Tebex's own words), as do the buyer's order page and **Your orders**; the Tebex page counts the ones running, overdue, ending and over. **A renewal is recorded as a payment of the order that started it**, once (found by its own transaction, the order's custom data or the recurring payment's reference), and never marks the order paid again or changes what it took in or what can be refunded; one that is not for what the order holds (other packages, more than ordered, another currency, no amount) is held for review with a critical log line and shows as Needs a look. A refund or a lost dispute of a renewal is noted on the order and leaves its refunds alone; a refund or dispute is found by its transaction only, so one about a renewal the shop never recorded is flagged and changes nothing instead of being taken for the order's first payment. A renewal held for review shows the currency Tebex took it in, and renewals are counted in the database so two processed at once are both counted. Asking to cancel does not end it (it runs to the end of what was paid for); Tebex's `ended` is when it is over, and the moment is kept for the entitlements slice. A delivery older than one already applied is ignored, since Tebex retries and nothing promises the order; one that names no shop order is not kept and says so. A guest's recurring payment moves to the account that claims the order. The copy is provider-neutral (`provider_subscriptions`, with an owner like orders and payments). Needs `php artisan migrate`; no new permission. **Entitlements** (what a purchase grants and takes back when it ends) were split into the next slice. Not yet seen from a real store (M2b): the order and shape of recurring deliveries, whether a renewal carries the order's custom data, and `recurring-payment.status-changed`. See [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#recurring-payments).
- **Entitlements (M2, seventh slice).** What a purchase gives an account in the shop, whichever provider took the money, as a provider-neutral layer. An **entitlement** is a **feature** (on or off) or a **limit** (a number; grants add up), with a key code asks for (`reports`, `team.seats`) that never changes. **Commerce → Entitlements** makes them; the new **Gives** column on **Commerce → Tebex** sets what each Tebex package gives (for a limit, how much one unit adds). They are given when an order is paid, once per order line and entitlement, to whoever owns the order (what is given is fixed when it is paid, so changing a package later does not reach earlier orders; a guest's order gives to nobody until an account claims it; a renewal gives nothing more) and **follow the money**, like downloads: taken back when the order is refunded in full or a dispute is lost, and when a recurring package's recurring payment ends (Tebex's `ended`, or a status of Expired or Cancelled; cancelling being asked for, or a failed renewal, takes nothing back), given back if the refund fails or the recurring payment is live again, and never touching one a person took back. Code asks with `Entitlements::for($owner)`, `$user->canUse('reports')` and `limitOf()`, the **`entitled:key[,key]`** route middleware (every key named is needed; naming none is an error rather than letting everyone in), the `auth.entitlements` page prop and `useEntitlements()`; nothing is cached, so an answer is never older than the last grant. Staff see what an order gave (and why one was taken back) on the order; customers see what their purchases give them under **Your orders**. Only the two kinds that need nothing else to work exist: a role, a badge or a download would change something outside this layer (and a package that could give a role is a way to escalate privileges), so each waits to be wired with its own allow-list. Stripe plans and the shop's own products are not sources yet. Needs `php artisan migrate`; no new permission. Also: a test now fails the build on any index name longer than MySQL allows (64), which SQLite never complains about. See [docs/entitlements.md](docs/entitlements.md).
- **Tebex Checkout API mode, first half (M2, eighth slice, EXPERIMENTAL).** `TEBEX_CHECKOUT_API_ENABLED=true` (with `TEBEX_PROJECT_ID` and `TEBEX_PRIVATE_KEY`) makes the Tebex provider sell **the shop's own products** instead of Tebex's packages, with Tebex only taking the payment as merchant of record. It is an alternative to the packages shop, not a companion: `/shop` stays the shop's window and the packages shop is off. Only products that need no shipping can be sold (one that has to be shipped cannot be added, and is refused at checkout if it is already in a cart), as single payments. Tebex works out the tax and applies its own codes, so the shop's codes, gift cards, shipping and tax are not used and no address is asked for. Checking out makes one `POST /checkout` to Tebex's Checkout API (HTTP Basic with the project id and private key; the same timeout, retry and secret-redaction rules as the Headless client; a call that changes something is not repeated after a server error) with each order line as a custom item that carries the order, the nonce and the line it is for. The webhook settles the order only when the payment is for **exactly the lines, quantities and prices ordered** (Tebex warns a webhook holds what was actually bought; a product that reports no readable price, or one in another currency, counts as a mismatch, since the total paid may differ from the order's by the tax Tebex adds); anything else is held for review. A cart of free products is paid on the spot, even before Tebex is set up. An order of the shop's products is not completed just because Tebex took the payment: the shop delivers it (downloads, licence keys) and it completes when that is all it owes. An earlier basket is closed (its rows taken out) before a new one replaces it. Off by default, never tried against a real account, and it needs Tebex's approval (not given to FiveM or RedM creators): see [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#selling-the-shops-own-products-the-checkout-api-experimental) for what is unverified. Refunds through the API, looking a payment up and the ACP page for this mode are the next slice. No migration; no new permission.
- **Tebex Checkout API mode, second half (M2, ninth slice, EXPERIMENTAL).** With `TEBEX_CHECKOUT_API_ENABLED` the shop can now **refund through Tebex**: **Refund** on the order page asks Tebex to return the payment (`POST /payments/{id}/refund`). Tebex takes no amount, so a payment goes back in full or not at all: providers have a new `partial_refunds` capability (Stripe has it, Tebex does not), a request for less is refused before any refund is recorded and the form's amount is fixed, and less than the whole can only be recorded as a refund made another way. The refund is known as `{transaction}-refund`, which is what Tebex's own `payment.refunded` delivery is recorded under, so the two are one refund. A refusal fails it; a pending refund stays pending until it goes through; and when Tebex cannot be reached or has a problem of its own the refund is left **pending and unconfirmed**, not repeated (it may have been made), until **Check refund** finds it, or Tebex's own `payment.refunded` delivery arrives (which settles that refund rather than leaving it pending while the customer has their money). A refund already asked for, and its **Check refund**, follow how the payment was made, so they keep working after the mode is switched off (starting a new refund is offered only while it is on). A refund made in the Tebex panel is found the same way. **Check payment** now works for these orders too: for a delivery Tebex gave up on, it asks Tebex for the basket's payment and applies it as the delivery would have been (same matching, same checks that it is for exactly what was ordered, same fulfilment). The **Tebex page** shows the Checkout API's connection (the end of the project id, whether the key is set; never the key), a **Test connection** button, and no catalogue; the catalogue sync, the `tebex:sync` command and the scheduler stand down in this mode. Still experimental, still never tried against a real account: see [docs/tebex.md](docs/tebex.md#refunds) for what is unverified. No migration; no new permission.
- **Subscription plans give entitlements (M3, first slice).** What a user can do now follows the plan they pay for. In **Billing → Plans**, each plan has a **Gives** column (a key icon opens the list to tick, the same dialog Tebex packages use; a limit says how much the plan gives, once; needs `billing.acp.edit`). Nothing is copied when someone subscribes: an account holds what the plans of its **live Cashier subscriptions** give, worked out each time it is asked, so changing a plan, swapping a subscription or letting it end reaches the account at once, with nothing to fall out of step. A subscription counts as Cashier says (active, on a trial, or cancelled but inside the period paid for; not past due, incomplete, unpaid or ended: `Cashier::keepPastDueSubscriptionsActive()` changes the past-due rule) and is matched to its plan by Stripe price. A plan and a purchase add up (limits sum, a feature is on if either gives it), and `$user->canUse()`, `limitOf()`, the `entitled` middleware, `auth.entitlements` and the "What your plan and purchases give you" card all include plans. The pricing page and Billing settings list what each plan gives (a limit with its amount) before the highlights staff wrote. Because the plan is what gives, deleting a plan, pointing it at another Stripe price and deleting an entitlement a plan gives are refused while people are subscribed to it. The seeded Starter and Pro plans give two example entitlements (`projects`, `priority-support`; see `config/billing.php`). One new table, `subscription_plan_entitlements`; run `php artisan migrate`. See [docs/entitlements.md](docs/entitlements.md#subscription-plans).
- **A failed subscription payment is followed up (M3, second slice: dunning).** A subscriber is now emailed when a renewal payment fails (for **each** failed attempt, saying what Stripe will do next: the date of the next try, or that it will not try again), when a payment that had failed goes through, and when Stripe ends the subscription for want of payment. Each email is sent **once** however often Stripe delivers the webhook or an admin replays it (a notice is claimed in the new `billing_dunning_notices` table before it is sent, and the claim is undone if sending fails); the first payment of a new subscription is not chased. Only a subscription's invoice is chased (a manual or one-off invoice is not), the emails report what was due or paid (not the invoice total, which a credit can exceed), and a failure Stripe delivers after the invoice was paid is ignored. The wording follows the past-due rule: by Cashier's default (kept) a subscription that is past due, unpaid or incomplete does not count as live, so **what the plan gives is paused from the first failed payment**, and the email and the billing page say so (they say the plan stays active if the app calls `Cashier::keepPastDueSubscriptionsActive()`, but never once Stripe has given up or the subscription is unpaid: the page follows whether the subscription is actually live). Because Stripe does not retry when a card is changed, the billing settings show a notice while a payment is behind, with **Update payment method** and **Pay now**: it asks Stripe to pay the latest invoice with the card on file and brings the plan back at once when the subscription, as Stripe then reports it, is no longer behind (a payment still processing is reported as such, not as received); a decline shows Stripe's reason, a card that needs 3-D Secure is sent to Stripe's page for the invoice, and an unreachable Stripe is reported and asked to be tried again (six presses a minute). A subscription that outlives its user no longer makes the webhook archive fail on a foreign key (Stripe would have sent that event for ever). One new table; run `php artisan migrate`. New [docs/billing.md](docs/billing.md).
- **Free trials (M3, second slice, part two).** A plan can give a new customer free days before the first payment: **Billing → Plans** has a *Free trial (days)* field (empty is none, up to 730; nothing is given away by default, and `config/billing.php` can seed it per plan). The customer adds a card as usual and Stripe charges nothing until the trial ends. **One trial per customer**: someone who has ever had a subscription (running, cancelled or ended) is charged from the start, checked under the per-user lock that starting a subscription and deleting an account already share, so two sign-ups at once cannot both get one. A trial counts as live, so the plan's entitlements are given from the first day. The pricing page and Billing settings show *N-day free trial*, *Start free trial* and that the card is not charged until it ends (only to someone eligible), and Billing settings show *Free trial until* a date and *Cancel trial*; cancelling during a trial charges nothing. Stripe's `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` (three days before the end) now emails the subscriber *Your free trial ends soon* with the date, the plan's regular price after it (with a line that any discount on the subscription is taken off, rather than a promised amount) and how to cancel, once, and not for a trial that was cancelled to end with it or has ended (a subscription set to end later is still warned). The once-only claim is now shared by the payment and trial emails (`BillingNotices`). One new column, `subscription_plans.trial_days`; run `php artisan migrate`, and switch `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` on in Stripe. See [docs/billing.md](docs/billing.md#free-trials).
- **Promotion codes on subscriptions (M3, second slice, part three).** A customer can type a Stripe **promotion code** when subscribing (Billing settings, and the pricing page once the payment form is loaded). *Apply* checks it at Stripe for the chosen plan before anybody pays and says what it takes off ("20% off for 3 months") or why it cannot be used: no live code of that name, one made for another customer, a flat amount in another currency, a coupon limited to other products (which Stripe would accept and take nothing off) or a flat amount that lasts forever. What Stripe holds a code to when it is used (first-time customers only, a minimum first payment) is shown as a note and left to Stripe; if Stripe refuses it when the subscription starts, the customer is told so and nothing is started or charged. Starting a subscription looks the code up again before the card is touched. Trying codes is limited to 10 a minute and 60 an hour per person, shared by checking a code and by starting a subscription with one. Codes are made and kept in Stripe; nothing about them is stored here. **Changed:** the subscribe requests took a raw Stripe coupon id as `coupon`; they take `promotion_code` now (on `settings/billing/subscribe` and `pricing/subscribe`), and starting a subscription on the billing page now refuses a plan that is not for sale, as the pricing page already did. No migration. See [docs/billing.md](docs/billing.md#promotion-codes).
- **A link to Stripe's customer portal (M3, third slice, part one).** The billing page has a **Manage billing in Stripe** button for a customer who has a billing account at Stripe. It makes a billing portal session (a POST, limited like the other billing requests) and sends them to Stripe, where they can update their card, see their invoices and, as far as your portal settings allow, change or cancel their subscription; it always comes back to the billing page, and what they change reaches the shop through the subscription webhooks that are already needed. Save the portal's settings in the Stripe Dashboard first, in each mode (until then Stripe refuses to open one: the customer is told plainly and the reason is logged). `BILLING_PORTAL_ENABLED=false` removes the button and the route; `STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION` names one of several portal configurations. No migration. See [docs/billing.md](docs/billing.md#customer-portal).
- **Metered usage (M3, third slice, part two).** A **meter** counts something a customer does, per calendar month, against what their plan allows. The one that comes with the shop counts **API calls**: every request made with an API token on a route that needs one is a use (a request for something that is not there too, but not one that failed on the server's side or was rate limited), Starter allows 1,000 a month and Pro 10,000 (the amount is a limit entitlement, `api-calls`, given by the plan), and a request past it is refused with a 429, `Retry-After` and `X-Usage-*` headers. An account whose plans give no allowance is counted but not limited unless you set `BILLING_API_FREE_ALLOWANCE`; `BILLING_API_OVERAGE=allow` counts past the allowance instead of refusing. Uses go in a `usage_events` ledger (an idempotency key makes a repeated use count once) through `App\Support\Usage\Usage::record()`, which is how to count anything else; a `UsageRecorded` event is the hook for forwarding to a Stripe billing meter. Customers see **Usage this month** on the billing page, and staff **Billing → Metered usage** (a month picker, daily chart and heaviest accounts). Events go with a deleted account, are in the data export, and are pruned after `BILLING_USAGE_RETENTION_MONTHS` (13) by a daily job that needs the scheduler. **Not built:** billing the overage through Stripe (a second, metered item would stop the plan matching its one Stripe price) and periods that follow each subscription's billing cycle. **Run `php artisan migrate`** (one new table); on an existing site run the plan seeder, or set the `api-calls` limit by hand, to give plans an allowance. See [docs/usage.md](docs/usage.md).
- **API keys (M3, fourth slice).** Anyone signed in can make API keys for themselves in **Settings → API keys**: each is limited to the scopes they tick (`profile:read`, `blog:write`, `polls:vote`, `forum:write`, `forum:moderate`, `support:read`, `support:write`, listed in `config/api.php`), expires after 30 days, 90 days, a year or never (the site can switch "never" off), shows its secret once, can be revoked or removed, and shows when it was last used and how many API calls it made this month. A person can have `API_KEYS_MAX` (10) keys they can still use, making them is rate limited, and where the site asks for a verified email address making one needs it. The ACP form for staff offers the same scopes (and full access). A person's data export lists their keys, never the secrets. The OpenAPI document says which scope each operation needs, read from the routes. The owner is **emailed whenever a key is made** on their account (in settings, by signing in over the API, by staff, or by rotation), never with the key itself and whatever their notification settings say. **Rotate** makes a new key like the old one (same name, scopes and limits, the same length of life from now) and lets the old one go on working for a grace period of 0, 1 hour, 1 day or 7 days; it needs room for both under the limit on keys, so rotating cannot pile up keys. A person can **limit a key's calls** an hour and a day when they make it or later, unless staff set its limits, which stay staff's (`limits_set_by_staff_at`). Two columns are added to `personal_access_tokens`: run `php artisan migrate`. See [docs/api-keys.md](docs/api-keys.md).
- **Admin audit log (M3, fifth slice, part one).** Every change somebody signed in makes in the admin area (a POST, PUT, PATCH or DELETE to an `acp/` address) is written to a new append-only `audit_logs` table: who, what it was done to, every account it concerns (a bulk ban is in the history of each person it banned, not only the first), what changed as it was and as it became (accounts, roles, permissions, system settings, plans, coupons, gift cards, orders, refunds and API tokens, with secrets hidden), where from, and whether it succeeded, failed or was denied. It recognises the address and not the route group, so a route added later is covered; a refused request is recorded as denied. Roles and permissions are recorded as what was really added and removed (`Audit::tracking()`). **Admin → Audit log** (new permission `audit.acp.view`) lists entries with filters, shows each one's changes as before and after, and opens for one account from Admin → Users → Edit. Entries are never changed or deleted by the application; `AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 0, keep for ever) and a daily `PruneAuditLogs` job are the only way they go. Run `php artisan migrate` and `php artisan db:seed --class=RolePermissionSeeder`. See [docs/audit-log.md](docs/audit-log.md). Read-only impersonation follows in part two.
- **Read-only impersonation (M3, fifth slice, part two).** **Admin → Users → Edit → View as this user** (new permission `users.acp.impersonate`; admins have it) lets staff see the site as one customer sees it, to help with a support case. It asks for a reason (a ticket number will do), takes the staff member to the customer's dashboard under a bar that shows who, why, and the time left (30 minutes, `IMPERSONATION_MINUTES`, then it ends by itself), and ends with **Stop viewing**, by signing out, or when the account is banned, deleted or made staff. Only customers can be viewed as: not staff, not oneself, not a banned account. **It is read only:** the customer is swapped in for each request and the staff member put back before the session is saved (no sign-in event, no "new device" email, nothing in the customer's list of sessions); every request that is not a GET is refused; and a GET runs in a transaction that is rolled back, so even what looking would change (`last_activity_at`, a counter) is not kept. It is closed by default: a page is served only if `config/impersonation.php` lists it, and a test fails when a GET route is in neither list. Kept from view: security settings (the page sends the customer's decrypted two-factor recovery codes), downloads, checkout and payment pages (they reach Stripe or Tebex, which a rollback cannot undo), sign-in and OAuth links, and the whole admin area and API. Every page opened, every refusal and the end of the view are written to the audit log by the staff member about the customer, before the page is shown (a page that cannot be recorded is not shown); the customer is not told. It applies to the whole browser session, so use a private window to keep working in the admin area. The cart shown is the customer's: a cart is found by the account or the browser session, and the session is the staff member's, so while viewing `CartManager::forRequest()` looks at the account alone. Run `php artisan db:seed --class=RolePermissionSeeder`. See [docs/impersonation.md](docs/impersonation.md).
- **Branded emails (M3, sixth slice, part one).** Every email the site sends (the order, refund, shipping, billing and API-key notices, the support, forum and blog ones, and the "verify your email" and "reset your password" mails Laravel sends itself) is now in one layout: the name of the site and a badge with its first letter (or a logo), buttons and links in the brand colour, a footer, a plain-text version with the same name and footer, and a dark version for mail apps that are dark (Apple Mail and the iOS and macOS apps follow it). Set `MAIL_BRAND_COLOR`, `MAIL_LOGO_URL` and `MAIL_FOOTER` (and `APP_NAME` and `APP_URL`) in `.env`; each is checked (a colour is a hex colour, a logo an `http(s)` address, the name and footer shown as text and never as markup), and the words on a button and the colour of links are chosen so that they can be read on whatever colour is set (white or black on a button, a lightened link in a dark mail app). The theme is a Blade view (`resources/views/mail/brand.blade.php`) and the layout is in `resources/views/vendor/mail`, with `markdown.theme` and `markdown.paths` added to `config/mail.php`. A test fails when a notification picks a layout of its own. See `docs/email.md`.
- **The "Get started" checklist (M3, sixth slice, part two).** A card at the top of a new customer's dashboard lists what is worth doing first: confirm the email address (where that is not needed to sign in), add a picture or a bio, turn on two-factor authentication, choose what is emailed, choose a plan (while plans are on sale), make an API key, and say hello in the forum (while it is on). Each step ticks itself from the account (nothing about the steps is stored, so what was done before the checklist existed is done), it shows how far along they are, and it goes when everything is done, when the person hides it (`users.onboarding_dismissed_at`, a new migration) or when the account is 30 days old (`ONBOARDING_DAYS`, which also keeps an upgrade from putting it in front of existing customers). `ONBOARDING_ENABLED=false` removes it and `config/onboarding.php` sets which steps and in what order. Staff viewing as a customer see it but cannot hide it. See `docs/onboarding.md`.
- **Owner resolution is tested (M3, sixth slice, part three).** `Ownership::resolveUsing()` is the one place 1.1 adds teams: it says which owners a user acts for and who owns what they create. Tests give a user a second owner (a stand-in for a team) and check that orders, saved addresses, recurring payments, payments and entitlements are seen, opened and added up through it on the pages as well as in the policies and queries, that the `user_id` of whoever acted grants nothing, that an owner of another kind with the same key is not the user, and that no policy of an owned model mentions `user_id` and no code finds one by it. New records go to the owner given for them, and `AddressBook::remember()` looks there for a copy before saving another. `docs/ownership.md` explains who owns what and what changes when teams arrive.
- **Warnings and bans (M4, first slice).** **Admin → Users → ⋯ → Moderate** (and **Warn or ban the author** on a forum report, which attaches what was reported) opens a member's moderation page: where they stand, everything done to them (newest first, with the note for staff and who did it), and the buttons. A **warning** is a notice with a reason: kept, emailed, readable by the member in **Settings → Account standing**, and with no effect on their account (no points, no automatic ban). A **ban** needs a reason and may end on a date (through the end of that day in the moderator's time zone): the member is signed out, and when they try to sign in with the right password they are told the reason and the date; a ban with an end date lifts itself the moment the member next signs in or makes a request, and by a job every minute (`LiftRunOutBans`). Lifting a ban is in the history too. Moderators can leave out the email (for a spam account). Nobody can moderate themselves, an administrator cannot be banned, and only an administrator can moderate staff. New permission `users.acp.warn`, and the `moderator` role now gets `users.acp.view`, `users.acp.warn` and `users.acp.ban` when the seeder is run again. History is the new append-only `moderation_actions` table (and `users.banned_until`, `ban_reason`); the member's data export has it (reason and date, not the staff note). `MODERATION_CONTACT` adds where to ask about a decision. Emails show what a moderator typed as words, never as links or formatting (`MailText`). See `docs/moderation.md`.
- **Automatic moderation of the forum (M4, second slice, part one).** **Admin → Forums → Automod rules** (new permission `forums.acp.automod`) sets rules that every new thread and reply from anybody who is not staff is checked against, on the site and through the API: **words and phrases** (any capitals, markup ignored, optionally whole words only), **links** (more than a number, or any link to a domain and its subdomains), **the same text again** and **too many posts in a few minutes** (held posts count, so a wave is caught from its fourth). Each rule is for everybody or only for accounts under some days old, and **holds** a post (kept out of sight of everybody but its author, who sees it marked "Awaiting review", and the moderators; nobody is notified, nothing is earned and the thread is not bumped) or **refuses** it (the member is told it looks like spam). A box tries a text against the rules, and each rule counts what it has caught. **Admin → Forums → Review queue** (new permission `forums.acp.review`, which `moderator` has when the seeder is run again, along with `forums.acp.view`) lists what is held, oldest first, with who wrote it and why it was held, and approves it (everything a post makes happen then happens, once, however many moderators press) or rejects it, one at a time or several, with a link to warn or ban the author with the post attached. The reply and thread forms carry a hidden field that a script fills in, as the blog's comments already did. The API answers `202` with `held: true` for a held post and `422` for a refused one. New tables and columns: `automod_rules`, and `held_at`, `held_reason` and `held_by_rule_id` on `forum_posts` and `forum_threads`; held replies are left out of every query by a global scope on `ForumPost`. Not built: checking edits, other places than the forum, telling the author, patterns. See `docs/automod.md`.
- **Clearing out a spammer (M4, second slice, part two).** A moderator can take down **everything a member posted that is showing**, in one go: on the member's moderation page (a **What they have posted** card with the counts and a **Take it all down** button), **together with a ban** (a tick on the ban form, single or bulk), or **for several members without a ban** (**Users → Bulk actions → Take down their posts**). Their **threads are unpublished** (the replies other members made in them go out of sight with them, and a moderator can publish a thread again), their **replies in other threads are deleted**, their **blog comments and product reviews are rejected**, and what of theirs was **waiting in the review queue is rejected**. Nothing of anybody else's is touched, what was already down stays as it was, and the reports about what came down are marked as reviewed. A reason is required and is kept, with the numbers, in the member's history (new `details` column and a `removal` type on `moderation_actions`); **the member is not told**, and it is not on their account page or in their data export. New permission `users.acp.cleanup`, which `moderator` has when the seeder is run again; the same rules as a warning decide who it may be done to, and it is in the audit log as `users.cleanup`. Not built: a button that undoes a whole clean-up, choosing what to take down, taking back reputation. See `docs/moderation.md`.
- **Public member profiles and a directory (M4, third slice).** **Members** in the header lists everyone who is not banned (newest, by name, or by reputation, and a search by name) and opens a profile: picture, bio, links, badges, reputation, and, while those parts of the site are on, their forum signature, public threads and replies, and how many blog comments they have on published posts. Not built: following someone, last seen, and listing product reviews. No migration and no new permission. See `docs/members.md`.

- A reply or thread is created in one place now (`ForumSubmissions`), and what follows a post that is out (the bump, mentions, followers, the live page, reputation) is `ForumPublisher`, shared by the site, the API and the review queue, where each of four controllers used to carry its own copy (about 280 lines fewer). The API read a mention with a looser pattern than the site (an email address such as `jo@example.com` mentioned a member called `example.com` there, and `a_@bob` mentioned `bob`); both use the site's now.
- Banning a member needs a reason (the single ban and the bulk one), and the quick **Ban** and **Unban** items in the Users list are one **Moderate** item that opens the member's page. `PUT acp/users/{user}/ban` and `unban` need `users.acp.ban` on the route, and an administrator cannot be banned.
- **Scopes are enforced (security).** A token's `abilities` used to be free text that no route looked at. Every API route that takes a key now names the scope it needs (`scope:` middleware, checked before the route's model binding so a key cannot probe for what exists), and a request that lacks it gets a 403 that names the scope. Tokens with `['*']` work as before; a token whose abilities name none of the site's scopes (for example `read`) can no longer call anything that needs one. Find and fix them in **Admin → Tokens** (see [Upgrading from before scopes](docs/api-keys.md#upgrading-from-before-scopes)) and run `php artisan api:docs`. In the ACP the abilities field is the scope picker, and an update that chooses nothing is refused (an empty list used to be saved as a token that does nothing, although the form said "leave empty for full access").
- `POST /api/v1/auth/token` only accepts abilities that are scopes the site has (or `*`), and not an empty list; `DELETE /api/v1/auth/token` revokes the key (it stays on the person's page as revoked) instead of deleting it. **A key made by signing in over the API without `abilities` is now least-privilege and ends:** it can only read the profile (`profile:read`, or whatever `API_SIGN_IN_SCOPES` says) and lasts 90 days (`API_SIGN_IN_EXPIRY_DAYS`), where it used to get full access that never ended. Clients that need more send the `abilities` they need; to keep the old behaviour set `API_SIGN_IN_SCOPES=*` and `API_SIGN_IN_EXPIRY_DAYS=0`. The response now lists `abilities`. Making a key by signing in now follows the same rules as making one in Settings: it counts toward the limit on keys (`API_KEYS_MAX`, so a client that signs in at every start and never gives its key back is refused with a 422 once it has ten), needs a verified address where the site asks for one, is rate limited (counting failed attempts too), and lasts the default lifetime where the site does not allow keys that never end.
- **The billing pages split their permissions by action (security).** Until now `billing.acp.view` was enough to create, change and delete subscription plans and to run a webhook delivery again, which makes it do what it does (mark an order paid, end a subscription). Looking still needs `billing.acp.view`; each change now needs its own permission, as in Commerce: `billing.acp.create` (a new plan), `billing.acp.edit` (change a plan, choose what it gives, replay a delivery) and `billing.acp.delete` (delete a plan). The screens only show the buttons a person may use. The `admin` role holds every permission and `editor` and `moderator` hold none of the billing ones, so a default install is unaffected; if you gave someone only `billing.acp.view` and they manage plans, add the other permissions in Access control. The permissions already exist, so there is nothing to re-seed. The plan form request no longer lets the admin, editor and moderator roles through on their name alone.
- A product can now be priced at 0 (a free product), and a discount code may cover a whole order: such an order is paid on the spot. Previously both were refused. A payment provider is only needed for an order that has something to pay. Stripe is no longer sent a line that costs nothing.
- The shop's catalogue and product pages no longer send exact stock counts to the browser (they were visible in the page data), and the placeholder wording ("Needs pricing", "Starter catalog page...") shoppers could see is replaced with proper copy. PHP's `gd` extension (with WebP) is needed for product pictures and is now installed in CI.
- The Commerce overview in the ACP no longer holds the old quick-create forms (they were replaced by the product, variant, price and stock screens), formats money in the shop's currency rather than always dollars, and counts only stock that can be sold in its on-hand figure.
- The revenue figure on the ACP Commerce page now counts only paid orders, less what was refunded (it used to add up every order, including unpaid and cancelled ones).
- Support attachments are stored on a private disk (`SUPPORT_ATTACHMENT_DISK`, default `local`) instead of the public disk. A migration moves existing files.
- All text filters and search use case-insensitive `whereLike`, so they behave the same on MySQL, PostgreSQL and SQLite.
- The brand name now comes from one place (`APP_NAME`); the logo, web manifest and home page no longer hard-code it.
- Default `APP_NAME` in `.env.example` is `MetaForge`.
- Only prices in the store currency (`COMMERCE_CURRENCY`) can be added to a cart, and checkout prices every line again from the catalogue instead of trusting the cart.
- The header cart no longer shows an estimated 7% tax and a flat shipping charge that were not real; it shows the subtotal and links to the cart.
- `billing_webhook_calls` is keyed by `(provider, external_id)` and records attempts and errors; a migration backfills existing rows. The stored `stripe_id` is kept for the admin screens.
- The Stripe webhook controller verifies signatures through a shared `StripeSignatureVerifier`; behaviour is unchanged.
- Products are shipped by default, so checkout now asks for a shipping address. Shops with no shipping zones configured are unaffected: orders still ship anywhere at no charge.
- Validation messages for addresses use plain field names ("The postal code field is required").

### Removed

- The unfinished `mysql_fulltext` search driver (it referenced FULLTEXT indexes that were never created). Meilisearch support is planned for 1.0.

### Fixed

- **Notification emails were never sent by a worker started the documented way.** Every notification queues its email on a queue called `mail` and its database and push copies on `default`, but `composer dev` / `composer dev:ssr`, the README and the Supervisor example in `docs/redis.md` started `queue:listen` / `queue:work` without `--queue`, which reads only the connection's own queue (`default`): the emails piled up in `jobs` (or Redis) and nothing reported it. They now start the worker with the new `php artisan metaforge:queue work|listen`, which runs Laravel's `queue:work` / `queue:listen` with the options you give it on the connection's own queue (`DB_QUEUE`, `REDIS_QUEUE`), then `default`, then `mail`. A plain `--queue=default,mail` would have replaced a site's own queue and stopped the jobs that name none, so the command looks it up. `docs/redis.md` says why. `tests/Unit/QueueConfigTest.php` collects every queue the app puts something on (`viaQueues()`, `onQueue()`, `#[Queue]`, `$queue`), fails if a worker command in `composer.json`, the README or the Supervisor example is not started through the command, and checks the list it reads with the connection's queue left on `default` and set to another one; `tests/Unit/QueueWorkerCommandTest.php` covers the command. **If you run a worker of your own** (Supervisor, systemd, Forge), start it with `php artisan metaforge:queue work` (the same options as `queue:work`), or give `queue:work` `--queue=` your `DB_QUEUE` / `REDIS_QUEUE` (if you set one), `default,mail`, and restart it; emails waiting from before are sent when it starts.
- **The notification bell showed a pushed alert as "Notification", and clicking it opened an error dialog.** Two faults. The header read an alert pushed over the websocket as if its title, text and link were under `data`, but Laravel pushes them at the top level, so every alert came out with no title, no link and the time "Just now" (`resources/js/lib/notifications.ts` reads both shapes now, and does not count an alert it already has). And the notice in the site and the alert for the browser were sent as two notifications with two ids, so the alert's id belonged to no stored row: marking it read, dismissing it or clicking it answered 404, which the bell showed as an error dialog, and the next page load replaced the alert with the stored notice, so it seemed to clear itself. `SplitDelivery` sends both parts under one id (community notifications, support tickets, data exports), and marking or dismissing an id that is not the member's, or not stored, now changes nothing and returns to the page. `docs/notifications.md` describes it.
- **Security:** `symfony/mime` is updated to 8.1.10 so a MIME parameter name can no longer inject email headers (CVE-2026-45070).
- The API gave `null` for the name of a thread's board (`board.name`): a board has a `title`, and the resource read a `name` that does not exist. It is the board's title now.
- A blog post still showed the comments a moderator had rejected (or that were waiting to be approved) in the first page of comments it opens with: only the list that pages through them and the API left them out. The post, and the "most commented" sorts of the blog list, the post's recommendations and the dashboard (which counted every comment, so a spam wave made a post popular), now use the approved comments only (`Blog::approvedComments()`).
- A banned member's request with an API key ended in a server error (`PreventBannedUser` called `logout()` on a guard that has none), and the first one that got further would have deleted the key. It is now refused with a `403` and the reason, and the key is kept, so that it works again when a ban with an end date runs out.
- **Security: revoking a token did not revoke it.** The ACP's Revoke button set `revoked_at`, and the page said access stopped at once, but nothing checked the column: Sanctum only knows about expiry, so a revoked token kept authenticating. A Sanctum callback now refuses it, and restoring a token (clearing the revocation) makes it work again.
- `DELETE /api/v1/auth/token` always answered 500: the controller declared a JSON response and returned an empty one, after deleting the key, and the activity log then failed to write a row for the key that was gone. It answers 204, as the API documentation says. (A PHPStan baseline entry had been hiding the wrong return type.)
- A person signed in with a session (the site's own pages) calling an API route that limits tokens crashed with a `TypeError` in `ThrottleTokenUsage`, which assumed a real token.
- The OpenAPI document had no poll endpoints; it now documents listing, showing and voting in polls.
- **Deleting an account now ends its Stripe subscriptions.** Deleting a user (their own Settings → Delete account, and the ACP's single and bulk delete) used to leave the Stripe subscription running, so the card was still charged each period for an account that no longer existed. Before the user is deleted, every subscription Stripe still holds for their customer is now cancelled at once (not at the end of the period, and without a proration credit). Stripe is asked what is live instead of the `subscriptions` table being trusted, as that table is only as current as the last webhook. The Stripe customer is kept, since its invoices are what tax records are checked against, and a user who never reached Stripe is deleted without contacting it. If a subscription cannot be cancelled (Stripe unreachable, an error, a refusal) the account is **not** deleted: the person stays signed in and is told to try again, an admin is told which user was kept (in a bulk delete the other users are still deleted and the ones left are listed), and the failure is logged with the Stripe customer and subscription. Trying again is safe, since what was already cancelled stays cancelled. A customer or subscription that Stripe no longer knows is taken as done. Starting a subscription and deleting the account cannot overlap: both take a per-user lock (`billing.locks`; it needs a cache store that supports locks, which is every one but `null`), the deletion holding it until the user is gone, so a subscription started at the same moment is either listed and ended with the rest or finds the account gone and is refused (a start that has to wait too long gets HTTP 423 and is asked to try again).
- **Security:** signing in over the API (`POST /api/v1/auth/token`) could be used to try passwords against any number of email addresses from one place, since its limit (5 a minute for each address from each place) was per address. A place is now also held to 20 requests a minute whatever addresses it names, every request counted, right or wrong; past that the answer is a `429` with a `Retry-After` header. An address that is already over its own limit is turned away first and costs the place nothing. Also, an `email` that was not text (`email[]=…`) made the limiter fail with a `500` before validation could answer; it is a `422` now.
- Registering, asking for a password reset link and resetting a password answered `500` when `email` was not text (`email[]=…`): their rate limiter turned it into a string before validation could refuse it. Each now answers with the form's own validation error, and requests like that share one allowance, so they are no way round the limit.
- Posting a blog comment answered `500` when `honeypot` or `captcha_token` was not text (`honeypot[]=…`): the spam check turned them into strings before the form was validated. It now answers `422` with an error on that field (a honeypot that is not text counts as filled in). The API's comment route already did, as it validates first.
- **Security:** deleting a single user in the ACP did not check `users.acp.delete` (only the screen hid the button), so any editor or moderator could delete any user by sending the request. It now needs the permission, as the bulk delete already did. The `admin` role is unaffected, and an editor or moderator who was given `users.acp.delete` can still delete; anyone else who relied on the gap needs the permission added in Access control (the permission already exists, so there is nothing to re-seed).
- **Security:** support-ticket attachments were reachable by anyone with the URL.
- **Security:** social sign-in trusted any email address a provider returned. Anyone could sign in as an existing account by giving Discord (which lets people keep an address they never confirmed) that account's address, and a new account made that way was marked verified. Google's `email_verified` and Discord's `verified` are now read, and an address the provider has not verified is never used to recognise an account or to mark a new one verified.
- `SupportTicketMessage::ticket()` and `SupportTicketAudit::ticket()` looked for a nonexistent `ticket_id` column and always returned `null`.
- **PostgreSQL:** the blog `scheduled` status was rejected by a CHECK constraint that the original migration never widened. A new migration (`2026_10_08_000100_allow_scheduled_blog_status_on_postgresql`) fixes both fresh and existing PostgreSQL databases; existing installations only need to run `php artisan migrate`.
- Rolling back the blog status migration now turns `scheduled` posts into drafts first, so the rollback no longer fails once any post has used that status.
- Moving legacy support attachments to the private disk now keeps a row on its original disk when the original file cannot be deleted, so the move is retried and reported instead of looking complete while a public copy remains.
- Carts that had become orders were still returned as the shopper's cart; only open carts are used now.
- Cart line totals are computed in integer minor units instead of floating point.
- Dialogs that add something (an address in the address book) no longer open pre-filled with the previously saved values: Inertia's `form.reset()` returns to the last *submitted* values, not blanks.
